## Scenarios Modelled

17 scenarios across 5 lever categories, ranked by margin impact.

### Pricing Levers

| Scenario | New Margin | Delta | Delta % |
|----------|------------|-------|---------|
| Price +2% | +₹2.13L | +₹3.18L | +2221% |
| Price +5% | +₹6.91L | +₹7.96L | +757% |

A 5% price increase converts the business from loss-making to +₹6.91L margin. Price is the highest-leverage single variable because it flows directly to margin with no additional cost.

### Support Cost Reduction

| Scenario | New Margin | Delta |
|----------|------------|-------|

| Support -25% | +₹5.89L | +₹6.95L |

A 15% reduction in support costs (achievable through self-service options, proactive outreach, or support tier limits for heavy users) turns the business profitable.

Delivery cost reduction through route optimisation or logistics renegotiation offers strong leverage. A 10% reduction already converts to positive margin.

### Order Strategy

| Scenario | New Margin | Orders Removed | % Volume Lost |
|----------|------------|----------------|--------------|
| Remove all loss orders | +₹22.3L | 88,090 | 44.0% |
| Remove deep loss only (<-₹50) | +₹12.0L | 14,732 | 7.4% |
| Remove Standard losses only | +₹11.2L | 61,249 | 30.6% |

Removing all loss-making orders produces the highest margin improvement but eliminates 44% of order volume — a significant operational and strategic decision. The deep loss removal variant (orders with margin below -₹50) removes only 7.4% of volume while capturing ₹12L in margin improvement. This is the most actionable order strategy lever.

### Channel Reallocation

Reallocating 30% of Paid Search budget to Organic improves margin by ~₹39K. This is relatively small because LTV differences between channels, while directionally significant, are modest in absolute terms at current order economics.

Channel reallocation becomes significantly more valuable once per-order economics are fixed — shifting acquisition toward Organic matters most when each order generates positive margin.

## Combined Scenario

Applying three levers simultaneously — **+5% price increase, -15% support cost reduction, remove deep loss orders** — produces:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| New total margin | +₹22.1L |
| Margin delta | +₹23.1L |
| New margin % | +13.9% |

This represents a return to the margin levels seen in early 2024, achieved without requiring aggressive volume growth.

## Strategic Conclusion

The business does not need more customers. It needs better economics on existing customers. The path to profitability runs through three levers in priority order:

1. **Reprice** — a modest price increase has the highest leverage and lowest operational complexity
2. **Reduce support cost** — target the top 10% of customers by support cost, who average -₹77 lifetime margin
3. **Fix Standard service** — either raise its price specifically or restructure its support model

Growth should resume only after unit economics are positive. Acquiring more customers at current economics accelerates losses.
