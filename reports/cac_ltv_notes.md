# CAC vs LTV Analysis Notes

## Summary

No acquisition channel recovers its customer acquisition cost within the 24-month observation window. The industry benchmark for a sustainable business is LTV:CAC ≥ 3.0x. Every channel in this analysis is below 0.1x.

---

## Channel-Level Results

| Channel | Customers | CAC | Avg LTV | LTV:CAC | Net Value | Status |
|---------|-----------|-----|---------|---------|-----------|--------|
| Organic | 6,078 | ₹71.58 | +₹5.01 | 0.07x | -₹66.57 | Critical |
| Referral | 3,305 | ₹87.40 | -₹10.60 | -0.12x | -₹98.00 | Critical |
| Social | 1,612 | ₹268.81 | -₹8.77 | -0.03x | -₹277.58 | Critical |
| Paid Search | 9,005 | ₹309.40 | -₹9.67 | -0.03x | -₹319.07 | Critical |

Organic is the only channel with positive LTV, but even Organic does not recover its ₹71.58 CAC within 24 months. The business would need to observe customers for significantly longer, or improve per-order margins, before any channel becomes economically viable.

---

## CAC Trend

Paid Search CAC rose approximately **50% from January 2024 to December 2025**, driven by increasing platform competition and cost-per-click inflation. This means the problem is not static — the economics of paid acquisition are deteriorating further even as LTV remains flat or negative.

Organic CAC remained relatively stable (~₹65–80 range), reinforcing it as the most defensible channel.

---

## Analytical Notes

- CAC uses first-touch attribution: 100% of channel spend attributed to customers acquired via that channel
- LTV = sum of contribution margin per customer across all observed orders
- Payback period defined as months until cumulative margin per customer exceeds CAC
- No channel reached payback within 24 months at current unit economics
- Multi-touch attribution would distribute credit differently but would not change the directional finding given the magnitude of the gaps

---

## Implication

Reallocating 30% of Paid Search budget toward Organic is estimated to improve total margin by ~₹39K — modest in absolute terms but directionally correct. The larger lever is improving per-order economics (pricing, support cost reduction) so that all channels generate positive LTV, making acquisition investment worthwhile regardless of channel.
