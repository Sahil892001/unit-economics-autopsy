# Executive Summary — Unit Economics Autopsy

## Overview
Despite strong revenue and order growth, the business is experiencing declining profitability. An end-to-end unit economics analysis was conducted to identify structural drivers of margin erosion and evaluate corrective actions.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Growth Masks Structural Losses
While average contribution margin appears positive, a significant share of orders are loss-making. Losses are concentrated in a small tail of extreme negative-margin orders, obscuring true profitability.

### 2. Acquisition Quality Has Degraded
Customer cohorts acquired through high-cost paid channels underperform on lifetime margin relative to acquisition cost. Organic and referral channels significantly outperform paid channels on an LTV-to-CAC basis.

### 3. Operational Complexity Drives Margin Erosion
Support-heavy customers and certain service types generate revenue but destroy margin due to outsized support and delivery costs.

---

## Scenario Insights

- A modest 5% price increase produces a meaningful uplift in contribution margin
- Eliminating or restructuring loss-making orders materially improves profitability
- Targeted reductions in support costs offer high leverage due to heavy-tail behavior

---

## Recommendations

1. Reallocate acquisition spend away from low LTV-to-CAC channels  
2. Introduce pricing or service constraints for support-heavy customer segments  
3. Focus growth on services and regions with demonstrated positive unit economics  

---

## Risks & Next Steps
- Continued growth without addressing unit economics risks compounding losses
- Future analysis should incorporate fixed cost allocation and churn dynamics
